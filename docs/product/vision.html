REVIEWED DESIGN ADAPTATION · SRC-010

Human-guided workflow evaluation

Turn one explicitly authorized, repeatable workflow into a testable candidate. Keep its owner, evidence and permission boundary visible from selection through rollback.

Design narrative, not a live service. This static page runs no collection, orchestration, provider calls, training, promotion or external action. It contains no live fleet, customer outcomes, measured savings or production benchmarks.

Refinement without default collection

The proposal is a reviewed second layer around agent-assisted work: select a bounded pattern only after its owner opts in, curate representative examples, compare programs and model choices, then seek separate approval for any release. Narrower implementations might reduce total cost while preserving delivery quality. That is a hypothesis to test, not a promised result.

Console would display attributable evidence. Tether would bind feedback to an immutable output ID, revision and digest; an edit creates a new output version rather than silently moving earlier feedback. Refinery would consume only separately authorized, reviewed examples. Reading a message, an operator correction or a successful task does not automatically authorize dataset collection.

Where the idea might apply

Repeatable assistance

Opted-in support triage, document tagging or meeting summaries could be narrowly scoped experiments. Source permission, redaction and acceptance criteria come first.

Agent coordination

Task-routing patterns could be compared under explicit review. Repetition does not create a schedule, a new credential grant or permission to control another session.

Opportunity research

Job, contract or consulting research are possible domains. Reading and evaluating are distinct from contacting someone, submitting a proposal or making a commitment, each of which needs its own authority.

Not all work reduces to compute. Human judgment, relationships, legal duties and physical work remain constraints. These domains are examples of research questions, not connected applications.

The controlled refinement loop

The seven stages describe intended requirements. They are not controls that this page can execute.

  1. Select and authorize

    Define one workflow, its owner, permitted inputs and effects, target account/session bindings, intended use, budget and stop conditions. A scope change needs fresh authorization; feedback cannot expand it.

  2. Review and redact

    Check source permission and remove sensitive material before any dataset entry. Preserve the immutable output identity, provenance and explicit exclusions. Quarantine conflicting or uncertain feedback instead of treating it as consent or truth.

  3. Curate a golden dataset

    Label representative cases, record adjudication and version the dataset. Keep related examples in the same stable train, validation or held-out split. Task-specific gold rules may admit examples only under explicit criteria. Gold eligibility is separate from serving approval.

  4. Choose candidates

    Compare a baseline with bounded DSPy program configurations and smaller-model options on the same task definition and evaluation set. Pin program, model, dataset and metric revisions. Choosing a candidate grants no provider budget or release authority.

  5. Evaluate

    Measure quality, safety failures, latency, total cost, human repair and uncertainty. Report sample sizes, exclusions, units and held-out results. A cheaper candidate must not advance below an agreed quality or safety threshold; missing evidence stays missing.

  6. Obtain human approval

    Present evidence and risks for the exact candidate and artifact digests. Evaluation passed, approved, promoted and serving are different facts. Approval must identify the permitted scope and rollback reference; experiments cannot promote themselves.

  7. Monitor and roll back

    For a separately authorized release, observe its approved scope and metrics. Stop or restore the prior approved revision when a gate fails, and reconcile uncertain effects before retrying. The existence of a rollback plan is not proof that recovery works.

Testable hypotheses

  • H1: Some bounded workflows may use smaller models without unacceptable quality loss.
  • H2: Adjudicated golden datasets may improve repeatability and reveal errors.
  • H3: Versioned artifacts, explicit approval and tested rollback may make experiments easier to govern.

Each needs permissioned evaluation. Historical demo numbers were simulated; they do not establish Runspindle performance. No numerical result or historical success claim is imported here.

Truthful console evidence

Keep planned, stub, synthetic, component, isolated integration and live acceptance distinct. A static page review proves document behavior only. It does not qualify a console runtime, desktop integration, restart recovery or production release.

A future operator view must show observation time, source, sequence, revision and freshness policy. An unavailable or stale feed is unknown, never an automatic live/healthy badge. Requested, acknowledged, observed terminal, validated and delivered states remain separate. Rendering a control or account label does not enforce backend permission or lease fencing.

Read the canonical repository's current implementation status and component evidence separately. No historical test result transfers to Runspindle.

Economics of accepted work

Divide total model, tool, human, verification, maintenance and recovery cost, including failed attempts, by verified authorized completions. With zero accepted completions, report cost and zero completions rather than a misleading unit price.

Fixed integration and evaluation costs can outweigh gains on low-volume tasks. Saved minutes are not automatically cash savings. Repeat comparisons when workload, model or platform changes. This design asserts no measured price, savings or break-even point.
